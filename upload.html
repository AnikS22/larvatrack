Upload a top-down clip of a petri dish, say how wide the dish is, and get how far each larva crawled. Processing runs on the lab machine, so there is no size or length limit — a five-minute clip takes about a minute.

Observed is the route actually seen. Least it walked adds the straight line across stretches where the larva was lost — the animal got from one end to the other, so that distance is a floor, not a guess. Speed is the steadier number: it holds to about 3% whether sampled at 2 Hz or 30 Hz, while the total depends on how much of the clip the larva was successfully followed for.
These are not comparable to published rover/sitter path lengths, which are measured on yeast paste where larvae feed and dwell. On bare agar they roam much further. Scale assumes a circular dish viewed square-on; a tilted camera biases every distance by roughly the cosine of the tilt.
