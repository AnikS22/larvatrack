Cold shock (paralysis & recovery)

Times how long flies on ice take to stop moving, and how long they take to wake up once they are warm again. Paralysis is reported in seconds from going onto ice; recovery in minutes from coming off it.

The biology

Flies are cold-blooded. On ice their nerves and muscles lose ion balance and they fall into a reversible paralysis, chill coma. Chill coma recovery time after warming is a standard measure of cold tolerance; it differs between genotypes, sexes and ages.

How to use it
  1. Upload the video and name the tube's genotype.
  2. Play or step through it and press each button as it happens: onto ice, 50% and 100% paralysed, off ice, 50% and 100% recovered.
  3. Fix any time by typing in its box. Leave a box empty if it never happened.
  4. Save the tube; Edit any saved tube later.
Reading the results

Paralysis times are seconds from going onto ice; recovery times are minutes from coming off it. Faster paralysis and slower recovery both mean less cold tolerance.

The statisticstime to 50% paralysis = t(50% paralysed) − t(onto ice)time to 50% recovery = t(50% recovered) − t(off ice)

Each tube is a replicate; bars show mean ± SD and Welch t-tests compare genotypes.

Tips & common mistakes
  • “Recovered” = standing upright, decided before you start.
  • Film until every fly recovers; otherwise the value is only a minimum.
  • Use the ±0.1 s buttons for exact moments.
