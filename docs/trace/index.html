Larval foraging (hand tracer)

← All FlyBench tools

Measure how far each Drosophila larva crawls by following it with the mouse while the clip plays back fast. Everything runs in your browser — the video is never uploaded.

The biology

The foraging (for) gene encodes a cGMP-dependent protein kinase (PKG). Larvae with the rover allele (forR, high PKG) crawl long paths while feeding; sitters (forS) stay put (Sokolowski, 1980; Osborne et al., 1997). The difference is clearest on food and shrinks on plain agar.

How to use it
  1. Open the plate video. Drag across the dish rim to rim and type its diameter.
  2. Press Follow with the mouse, click the larva, and keep the pointer on it while the clip plays fast. Keys 1–5 switch larva; space pauses.
  3. Redo any larva to correct it. Results list every larva's distance.
Reading the results

Path length in millimetres over the clip. Rovers should exceed sitters, most on yeast.

The statisticsmm per pixel = dish diameter (mm) ÷ dish width (px)path length = Σ distance between successive pointer positions

Each larva is a replicate; compare genotypes with a Welch t-test (e.g. in the stress tool's spreadsheet, or your own).

Tips & common mistakes
  • A wrong diameter scales every number wrongly with no other symptom.
  • Keep the camera fixed.
